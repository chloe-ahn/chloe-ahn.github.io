I am on the 2026–27 academic job market.

I am a macroeconomist working on household finance, housing, public finance and inequality. I study how the economic environment and public policy shape what households own and save over their lives, and who bears the resulting gains and losses. I am also interested in how household saving interacts with asset prices and long-term interest rates.

Job Market Paper

Priced Out: Housing Costs, Generational Wealth Gaps, and Youth Welfare

[PDF]  latest version October 2026  [Abstract]

Each US birth cohort from 1940 to 1980 has owned less housing than the one before it. In the Panel Study of Income Dynamics, ownership among household heads aged 25 to 34 fell from 53 percent in the 1940 cohort to 31 percent in the 1980 cohort. I estimate a life-cycle model of saving, tenure and housing quality on the 1940 cohort, then hold preferences fixed and let each later cohort face the house prices, interest rates and earnings of its own time. The changing environment alone produces a fall in ownership of about the size seen in the data. House prices account for the largest part of the ownership gap, while earnings account for slightly more of the welfare loss, which falls hardest on households with low income and little wealth.
